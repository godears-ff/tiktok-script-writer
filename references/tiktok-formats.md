# TikTok Video Formats & Hook Strategies (US Market)

## Duration Standard

| Mode | Default | Supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General | 80 seconds, about 190-210 spoken words | 15, 30, 45, 60, or 80 seconds |
| Commentary | 80 seconds, about 190-210 spoken words | 15-30 seconds on request; otherwise 45-90 seconds |
| Social news | 60-80 seconds | 30, 60, or 80 seconds |

The user's requested duration wins. This table is the source of truth across
the skill; the format examples below describe common uses, not mandatory
lengths.

## Hook Strategies (First 3 Seconds)

Use one core idea. Spoken hooks normally run 8-12 words. A visual, evidence,
or result hook can replace or support the spoken hook.

### The Curiosity Gap
State something incomplete that MUST be resolved.
> "I found the one habit that's destroying your gains."
> "This one app just saved me $400 a month."
> "You're brushing your teeth wrong."

### The Bold Statement / Hot Take
Say something most people disagree with but can't ignore.
> "Your 9-to-5 is the reason you're broke."
> "Stop being nice in relationships."
> "I don't care about your 5-year plan."

### The Pattern Interrupt
Start mid-sentence, do something unexpected, or break the expected format.
> [Silent stare for 1 second, then] "That was awkward. Now here's why."
> "Can we talk about the elephant in the room?"
> [Start with a weird sound effect or jump cut]

### The Question Hook
Ask a question the viewer MUST answer internally.
> "When was the last time you actually felt proud of yourself?"
> "Why do Americans spend $1,200 a year on stuff they don't need?"
> "How much do you think this cost me?"

### The Direct Challenge
Tell the viewer they're wrong or missing something.
> "I bet you can't go 24 hours without this."
> "If you think you're good at [X], watch this."
> "Stop doing [common mistake]. Here's why."

### The Visual Hook
Show something visually shocking or fascinating before speaking.
> [Show a dramatic before/after]
> [Show a weird object or food]
> [Show a money stack, transformation, or satisfying clip]

### The News Result Hook
Lead with the verified outcome, then create a question about how it happened.
> "Three people were found dead in a submerged Jeep."
> "A homeowner was sentenced to 15 years after shooting three intruders."
> "Police say the clerk acted in self-defense."

### The Evidence Hook
Begin with a verified quote, document, recording, or visible detail, then
explain why it matters.
> "One line in the police report changed the case."
> "The surveillance footage shows who moved first."
> "This is the text message investigators focused on."

### The Legal Reversal Hook
Set up the gap between the initial incident and the legal outcome.
> "He called it self-defense. A jury disagreed."
> "She was arrested, but the video tells a different story."
> "The charge was serious. The final verdict was not what people expected."

## Niche-Specific Strategies

### Finance / Money
- Hook: Dollar amounts, income transparency, "wealthy people don't tell you"
- Tone: Authoritative, slightly condescending, no-nonsense
- Angles: "I made $X doing Y," "X habits keeping you poor," "the math doesn't lie"

### Fitness / Health
- Hook: Myth-busting, transformation, "everything you know is wrong"
- Tone: Hype, urgent, or brutally honest
- Angles: "Stop doing [X exercise]," "the real reason you're not seeing results," "30-second fix"

### Dating / Relationships
- Hook: Controversial opinion, "unpopular truth," gender-specific take
- Tone: Sassy, direct, or painfully honest
- Angles: "Men/Women settle for less," "you're the problem," "3 signs they're not interested"

### Productivity / Self-Improvement
- Hook: "The one thing," efficiency hacks, mindset shift
- Tone: Calm, authoritative, or hype
- Angles: "The 5-second rule changed my life," "why busy people get nothing done," "Atomic Habits in 60 seconds"

### Comedy / Entertainment
- Hook: Relatable situation, unexpected twist, absurd premise
- Tone: Casual, exaggerated, deadpan
- Angles: POV sketches, "nobody talks about this but," "shit Americans actually say"

### Food / Cooking
- Hook: Unexpected ingredient, "life-changing" recipe, speed
- Tone: Warm, excited, or competitive
- Angles: "World's best [dish] in 30 seconds," "the ingredient chefs hide from you"

## Timing Hypotheses (Verify Before Using)

These are content-testing hypotheses, not platform guarantees:

- **Sunday evening** — motivational / productivity content peaks
- **Monday** — work-related, back-to-routine, finance
- **Wednesday** — "hump day" humor, mid-week motivational
- **Friday** — "weekend mode," goofy, relatable work escape
- **Seasonal hooks** — use upcoming holidays (4th of July, Thanksgiving, Halloween, New Year) 2–3 weeks ahead
- **Current events** — use only when the current status and publication date
  were verified. Do not repeat a stale "breaking" claim or imply recency
  without checking.

## Freshness Gate

- Treat trends, slang, sounds, memes, pop-culture references, and platform
  features as time-sensitive.
- Verify the current status before using a specific trend, sound, challenge, or
  public figure.
- If verification is unavailable, write an evergreen script and omit the
  trend-specific element.
- Do not claim a trend or editing pattern will cause virality.

## Audio and Visual Patterns to Test

- **Audio**: use original, licensed, or platform-approved audio. Check whether
  the sound is current before treating it as a trend.
- **Text overlay**: bold white or yellow font with black outline, single word or short phrase per frame
- **Captions**: auto-generated + manually edited for emphasis (all caps for punchlines)
- **Jump cuts**: fast pacing, no dead air, cut every 2–5 seconds
- **Close-up / direct eye contact**: builds trust and urgency
- **B-roll**: screen recordings, stock footage, or second camera for context
- **CTA overlay**: arrow pointing to follow button, "save this" text flash

## US Audience Behavior
- The opening seconds decide whether viewers continue. Optimize the hook,
  pacing, and payoff instead of relying on unsupported attention-span numbers.
- Content that triggers "saving" behavior: hacks, templates, educational lists, recipes, financial tips
- Content that triggers commenting: hot takes, debated topics, "tag a friend who ___"
- Content that triggers sharing: relatable humor, shocking facts, "I needed to hear this"
- Duet/Stitch bait: leave a clear opinion gap for others to fill
