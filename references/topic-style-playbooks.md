# Topic-Style Playbooks

Use this reference after selecting the script mode. Choose one controlling
playbook, then adjust without replacing the factual and safety rules.

## How to Route

1. Identify the subject: news, crime, celebrity, music, sports, film,
   technology, business, health, lifestyle, food, viral, or politics.
2. Choose the highest-risk applicable playbook if the story crosses topics.
3. Set the primary viewer.
4. Pick the hook style.
5. Set pacing and evidence density.
6. Choose a CTA that fits the topic.
7. Match the visual language to the subject.

## Breaking News / Public Safety

- Viewer: people following an active event or public-safety issue
- Tone: calm, direct, urgent without panic
- Hook: verified outcome, new development, warning, or unresolved question
- Structure: result -> confirmed facts -> timeline -> limits -> next update
- Evidence: official statements, dates, jurisdiction, source links
- Pacing: fast facts, clear resets, no speculation
- Visuals: maps, documents, timelines, blurred sensitive footage
- CTA: what should officials clarify or release next?
- Avoid: panic, rumor stacking, graphic details, naming unconfirmed suspects

## True Crime / Courts

- Viewer: case followers and legal explainer audiences
- Tone: measured, suspenseful through sequence and evidence
- Hook: reversal, contradiction, key document, or unanswered legal question
- Structure: hook -> legal stage -> evidence -> dispute -> limits -> next step
- Evidence: court records, filings, testimony, official statements
- Pacing: deliberate; distinguish each legal stage
- Visuals: case timeline, document excerpts, anonymized evidence
- CTA: which legal standard or fact matters most?
- Avoid: armchair verdicts, graphic injury detail, treating allegations as proof

## Celebrity / Creator Gossip

- Mode: automatically use `Celebrity explainer series` when the source is
  primarily about the person's decision, relationship, deal, career, money,
  image, statement, controversy, or timeline. Read
  `celebrity-explainer-series.md`.
- Viewer: pop-culture followers who understand the personalities
- Tone: conversational, skeptical, lightly playful
- Hook: contradiction, direct quote, shock reveal, or messy timeline
- Structure: question hook -> answer axis -> three contrast layers ->
  receipts or qualifiers -> judgment -> decision CTA
- Evidence: screenshots, direct posts, statements, source attribution
- Pacing: quick, but pause at each major claim
- Visuals: name cards, blurred social posts, timelines, reaction graphics
- CTA: messy, believable, or pure promo?
- Avoid: presenting rumors as fact, sexual shaming, platform logos, fake
  texts, private-motive certainty, and body-based judgment

## Music / Hip-Hop News

- Viewer: genre fans, chart watchers, and industry followers
- Tone: energetic, informed, competitive
- Hook: chart result, surprise collaboration, legal turn, or industry conflict
- Structure: result -> context -> stakes -> big detail -> what comes next
- Evidence: chart data, release dates, credits, direct quotes
- Pacing: beat-driven; one strong fact per cut
- Visuals: charts, album cards, waveforms, release timelines
- CTA: does this change the artist’s run or legacy?
- Avoid: treating sales estimates as audited numbers or chart peaks as cultural proof

## Sports

- Viewer: team fans, fantasy players, and casual sports viewers
- Tone: energetic, knowledgeable, competitive
- Hook: result, upset, record, injury update, or matchup tension
- Structure: result -> key play/stat -> context -> implications -> next game
- Evidence: score, minutes, stats, standings, official updates
- Pacing: fast cuts on plays and numbers
- Visuals: scoreboards, stat cards, field/court diagrams, highlight placeholders
- CTA: prediction, roster decision, or player comparison
- Avoid: unsupported injury diagnoses and overreacting to one game

## Film / TV / Entertainment Commentary

- Viewer: fans of the title, genre, cast, or creator
- Tone: fan-native, opinion-led, scene-specific
- Hook: theory, emotional reaction, overlooked detail, or strong take
- Structure: hook -> scene context -> interpretation -> evidence -> counterpoint
- Evidence: scene details, dialogue, production choices, creator statements
- Pacing: clip-aware, but never copy source subtitles
- Visuals: shot descriptions, storyboard, cast/crew cards, motif graphics
- CTA: agree/disagree or which interpretation wins?
- Avoid: plot summary, spoilers without warning, unlicensed clip reliance

## Technology / Product Explainers

- Viewer: mainstream users or niche product buyers
- Tone: clear, practical, curious
- Hook: surprising capability, risk, price, or real-world consequence
- Structure: hook -> what it is -> what changed -> why it matters -> limits
- Evidence: demos, specs, tests, official documentation, current pricing
- Pacing: explain one concept per beat
- Visuals: screenshots, diagrams, before/after, plain-language analogies
- CTA: would you use it, or which tradeoff matters most?
- Avoid: jargon stacking, fake benchmarks, and treating demos as final products

## Business / Entrepreneurship

- Viewer: operators, founders, side hustlers, and business-news readers
- Tone: practical, skeptical, numbers-aware
- Hook: valuation, failure, money move, or strategic contradiction
- Structure: money/result -> business model -> risk -> strategic takeaway
- Evidence: revenue, contracts, filings, timelines, direct statements
- Pacing: steady and analytical
- Visuals: charts, org maps, deal timelines, product shots
- CTA: smart move or unsustainable bet?
- Avoid: treating projections as guaranteed results or revenue as profit

## Health / Mental Health

- Viewer: people seeking context, support, or responsible reporting
- Tone: empathetic, measured, non-diagnostic
- Hook: new study, personal statement, warning, or access issue
- Structure: verified update -> context -> limits -> practical support
- Evidence: medical bodies, peer-reviewed research, primary statements
- Pacing: slower, with clear qualifiers
- Visuals: simple explanatory graphics, resource cards, no graphic imagery
- CTA: what support or information should people have?
- Avoid: diagnosing, fear-based hooks, treatment promises, exploitation

## Fashion / Beauty / Lifestyle

- Viewer: style followers and product-curious viewers
- Tone: visual, confident, conversational
- Hook: object detail, price, transformation, or strong opinion
- Structure: look/detail -> why it works -> alternatives -> takeaway
- Evidence: price, materials, styling context, credited designers
- Pacing: visual-first, text-off friendly
- Visuals: close-ups, swatches, comparisons, red-carpet breakdowns
- CTA: would you wear it, or worth the price?
- Avoid: body shaming, counterfeit promotion, false scarcity

## Food / Travel / Local Stories

- Viewer: local viewers, food lovers, and travelers
- Tone: sensory, warm, experiential
- Hook: texture, price, location, tradition, or unexpected result
- Structure: sensory hook -> place/person -> experience -> context -> recommendation
- Evidence: prices, locations, ingredients, direct observations
- Pacing: sights and sounds lead; narration supports
- Visuals: close-ups, maps, preparation shots, ambient audio
- CTA: would you try it, or where should we go next?
- Avoid: claiming health effects, treating taste as objective fact, unverified origins

## Viral Internet / Memes / Wholesome Stories

- Viewer: broad TikTok audience
- Tone: playful, warm, fast, self-aware
- Hook: visual gag, unexpected kindness, contradiction, or meme template
- Structure: moment -> why it spread -> context -> payoff -> bigger meaning
- Evidence: original post, visible detail, creator response
- Pacing: punchy and loop-friendly
- Visuals: reaction graphics, sound cues, before/after, comment highlights
- CTA: tag, share, vote, or apply the template
- Avoid: manufacturing wholesomeness, exploiting real people for likes

## Politics / High-Sensitivity Public Issues

- Viewer: civic audience, affected groups, and news followers
- Tone: restrained, precise, source-heavy
- Hook: verified decision, consequence, contradiction, or public impact
- Structure: decision -> who is affected -> evidence -> disagreement -> what remains
- Evidence: primary documents, official statements, multiple sources
- Pacing: slower than entertainment content
- Visuals: maps, document excerpts, neutral timelines
- CTA: what should decision-makers answer next?
- Avoid: partisan slogans, unverified clips, harassment, dehumanizing language

## Style Switching Rules

- One controlling playbook per script.
- Add a second playbook only as a tone layer.
- Never use entertainment slang for death, abuse, health, or legal outcomes.
- Do not turn high-sensitivity stories into binary voting games.
- Keep the same core facts across style variants.
- When producing multiple styles, label them: `News`, `Fan`, `Analytical`,
  `Comedy`, or `Commentary`.
