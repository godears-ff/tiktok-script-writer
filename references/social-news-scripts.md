# Social News / True-Crime Scripts

Use this reference for breaking news, public-safety incidents, police and court
stories, viral surveillance footage, true-crime updates, and real-case
commentary. The goal is retention and shareability without turning accusations,
allegations, or viewer assumptions into facts.

## Freshness Gate

- Establish the current status of the case or incident before drafting:
  active investigation, arrest, charge, trial, verdict, appeal, correction, or
  closure.
- Treat every date, age, charge, injury, death toll, legal stage, and public
  statement as time-sensitive.
- Record the verification date and label the script with an `As of` date.
- Recheck the primary source immediately before publication. If the status
  changed, update the script rather than preserving an outdated hook.
- A viral clip, screenshot, repost, or edited compilation is not sufficient
  evidence by itself. Verify provenance and the original context first.

## Source Integrity

Prefer primary sources: official releases, court records, transcripts,
first-party documents, or direct statements. Secondary reporting can provide
context but should not be the only basis for a disputed legal claim.

For each material fact, record:

1. The exact claim or wording.
2. The source publisher and source type.
3. A direct URL or record identifier.
4. The source publication date.
5. The date the creator verified it.

If sources conflict, present the disagreement. If a source cannot be opened,
is anonymous, or cannot be traced to the original publisher, label it
unverified instead of repeating it as fact.

## Input Modes

| Input | Allowed output | Required limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full article, official release, court record, or transcript | Fact-grounded script | Cite the direct source, publication date, and unresolved points |
| Headline or title only | Draft outline with an angle and placeholders | Do not invent names, ages, charges, injuries, motives, or outcomes |
| Social post or viral clip | Context draft | Verify the original source before presenting it as news |
| Multiple conflicting sources | Comparison script | State where sources disagree and avoid declaring a winner without evidence |

## Build a Fact Ledger First

Before writing the hook, classify the material:

| Category | Meaning | Source requirement | Language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Supported by an official release, court record, or clearly identified primary source | Direct source, date, and verification date required | "Police said", "court records show", "the video shows" |
| Alleged | Claimed by a person or authority but not established | Name the claimant and source | "alleged", "accused of", "investigators suspect" |
| Disputed | Two sides or sources disagree | List both positions and sources | "the defense argues", "the family claims", "the accounts differ" |
| Unknown | Not yet established | State who has not released or confirmed it | "has not been confirmed", "authorities have not released" |

For title-only input, write the script as a structured draft. Mark every fact
that still needs verification.

## Required Sources Section

Every social-news output must end the English portion with a `Sources` section
that lists the publisher, source type, title, direct URL or record identifier,
publication date, verification date, and the facts supported. Do not use a
search-result page or unattributed repost as the direct source.

When no independent source exists, state:

`Source basis: user-supplied material only; not independently verified`

## Choose One Angle

Use one primary angle so the script does not become a vague event summary:

1. Straight news: what happened, what is confirmed, and what comes next.
2. Public safety: what viewers can learn or prepare for.
3. Legal analysis: what the charge, defense, or ruling actually means.
4. Hero or accountability: who acted, what they did, and what remains disputed.
5. Investigative timeline: how the evidence changed the official account.

The angle should be visible in the first sentence and the final question.

## Default 60-80 Second Structure

| Beat | Time | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0-3s | Lead with the verified outcome, evidence, or legal reversal |
| Fact anchor | 3-8s | Who, where, when, and the confirmed status |
| Context | 8-20s | Relationship, background, or sequence needed to understand the event |
| Escalation | 20-36s | The key actions, evidence, or turning point |
| Consequence | 36-50s | Injury, arrest, charge, verdict, sentence, or investigation status |
| Limits | 50-65s | What is alleged, disputed, or still unknown |
| CTA | 65-80s | Ask about the legal standard, public-safety lesson, or key evidence |

This table shows the 80-second version. Compress context and escalation for 60
or 30 seconds, but never cut the limits, source check, or legal qualifiers.

## Hook Rules

Use a hook that the source can support:

- Result hook: "Three people were found dead..."
- Evidence hook: "The surveillance video shows..."
- Legal reversal hook: "Police called it self-defense. A court disagreed."
- Accountability hook: "The warning was documented months earlier."
- Safety hook: "The first mistake happened before anyone entered the water."

Avoid unsupported claims such as "complete footage", "the full truth", or
"everyone missed this" unless the source genuinely establishes them.

## Legal-Language Rules

- Arrest is not a charge, and a charge is not a conviction.
- A lawsuit is civil unless the source explicitly describes criminal charges.
- Prosecutors file criminal charges; families normally file civil lawsuits.
- Use "alleged", "accused", "charged", or "suspect" until the legal stage
  supports stronger language.
- Do not use "murderer" before a conviction. Use "defendant" or "suspect" as
  the source supports.
- Do not say "self-defense" as a conclusion unless an official or court made
  that determination.
- State the jurisdiction and legal stage when they affect the outcome.

## Sensitive-Content Rules

- Avoid unnecessary names, ages, workplaces, schools, or locations for minors
  and victims.
- Do not repeat graphic death, injury, sexual abuse, self-harm, or suicide
  details just to increase shock.
- Use a content warning or restrained language when the subject requires it.
- Do not present speculation, reenactments, or edited clips as evidence.
- Do not invite viewers to guess a victim's actions or blame a person for
  being harmed.
- Prefer accountability, safety, and verified information over revenge framing.
- Do not fabricate or visually alter documents, surveillance footage, evidence
  photos, or reenactments to make a claim look stronger.

## Engagement Patterns

| Audience response | Best trigger | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Likes and shares | Clear moral resolution or satisfying reversal | "Police reviewed the video and ruled it self-defense." |
| Shares | Public-safety urgency | "The warning signs were documented before the crash." |
| Comments | Genuine legal or ethical ambiguity | "When does self-defense stop being self-defense?" |
| Saves | Ongoing case or complex explainer | "Here is the confirmed timeline so far." |

Do not force controversy when the source is clear. Use the CTA that matches
the actual uncertainty.

## CTA Patterns

- "Where should the line be drawn between self-defense and excessive force?"
- "What detail should investigators focus on next?"
- "Would a warning have changed the outcome?"
- "What should bystanders do when a situation escalates?"
- "Which fact is still missing from the public record?"

Avoid "What do you think happened?" when it invites uninformed accusations.

## Anti-AI News Language

Prefer plain spoken sentences:

- "Police say..." instead of "It has been reported by authorities that..."
- "The video shows..." instead of "The footage appears to depict..."
- "He was charged, not convicted." instead of "This raises serious questions."
- "The case is still open." instead of "The situation remains fluid."

Avoid vague filler such as "in a shocking turn", "this changes everything",
"the internet is divided", and "only time will tell."

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Current status and `As of` date are accurate.
- [ ] Every name, age, role, and gender is consistent.
- [ ] Arrest, charge, lawsuit, verdict, and sentence are not confused.
- [ ] Confirmed facts and allegations are visibly separated.
- [ ] Every material fact maps to a source in the Sources section.
- [ ] Source publication and verification dates are present.
- [ ] The source supports the hook and title.
- [ ] The script states what remains unknown.
- [ ] Sensitive content is restrained and necessary.
- [ ] The CTA asks a useful question rather than inviting harassment.
- [ ] English and Chinese versions preserve the same legal limits, dates, and
  source caveats.
- [ ] AI-generated or materially altered realistic media is labeled when
  required by the platform.

## Chinese Translation Rules

- Translate into precise, restrained standard Chinese, not entertainment
  slang.
- Keep `alleged`, `charged`, `suspect`, `disputed`, `not confirmed`, and
  similar qualifiers at the same strength as the English.
- Preserve case status, jurisdiction, dates, source attribution, and `As of`
  context.
- Do not translate "police say" as an established fact or turn an arrest into
  a conviction.
- Translate the Fact and Risk Notes, Sources, and Cover Prompt completely.
