# Viral Copy Review

Use this reference when the user asks for a stronger hook, more engaging
short-form copy, a rewrite, or a diagnostic review. Treat it as an editing
layer over the existing mode rules.

It can improve retention and interaction. It does not guarantee virality, and
it must never override factual accuracy, legal limits, privacy, or source
attribution.

## Four Diagnostic Checks

### 1. Hook

The first 0-3 seconds should contain one dominant idea: a real contradiction,
conflict, evidence detail, emotional moment, visceral image, or concrete
result.

Prefer:

- "She wasn't dead."
- "Twelve feet. 'I'm fine.'"
- "$2.2 million in two weeks."
- "A video is not proof."

Avoid:

- "Today we are talking about..."
- "Here is another update about..."
- A vague question that could fit any story.

For news and legal content, the hook must be supported by the fact ledger.
Never create conflict by implying a claim is proven when it is only alleged.

## Audience and Promise

Before writing the hook, identify:

- One primary viewer: casual fan, stan, true-crime viewer, sports fan, local
  audience, industry follower, or another specific group.
- One promise: what the viewer will understand, feel, or learn by the end.
- One central question: the exact tension the video resolves or advances.

If the script tries to deliver several unrelated promises, cut until one
dominant throughline remains.

When optimizing, draft at least three hook variants:

1. Outcome hook
2. Conflict hook
3. Evidence or contradiction hook

Choose the strongest one. Frame testing as a hypothesis, not a guaranteed
growth mechanism.

### 2. Language

Remove encyclopedia and press-release tone while preserving attribution.

Instead of:

- "According to reports, the individual allegedly..."

Prefer:

- "Police say..."
- "The report claims..."
- "Here's where the facts split."
- "The internet ran with one version. The record says something else."

The rewrite may be more conversational, but the legal stage and uncertainty
must remain intact.

### De-Newsroom / De-Wikipedia Pass

Convert written-report voice into spoken US TikTok voice:

| Written voice | Spoken TikTok voice |
| --- | --- |
| "On September 12, the organization announced..." | "Here's the part that changed everything..." |
| "The report alleges that..." | "The report claims..." or "Police say..." |
| "The individual was subsequently detained..." | "Then he was taken into custody." |
| "The incident resulted in..." | "That's when things went sideways." |
| "According to available information..." | "Here's what we actually know." |
| "The decision generated criticism..." | "Cue the backlash." |

Rules:

- Use contractions and short sentences, but vary sentence length.
- Name the actor when the source supports it; avoid passive constructions.
- Replace formal transitions with natural spoken bridges.
- Translate legal and institutional language into plain English while keeping
  the legal stage exact.
- Do not use slang, jokes, or false certainty in death, abuse, health, minor,
  or ongoing criminal cases.
- Do not copy tabloid language when the source only supports a rumor.

### Topic Voice Calibration

| Topic | Voice |
| --- | --- |
| Breaking news | Direct, calm, high-clarity |
| True crime / court | Measured, factual, suspenseful without exploitation |
| Celebrity / creator drama | Conversational, skeptical, lightly playful |
| Sports | Energetic, competitive, quick transitions |
| Film / TV / music | Fan-native, opinion-led, scene-specific |
| Technology | Plain-English explainer, analogy-driven |
| Health / mental health | Empathetic, cautious, non-diagnostic |
| Minors | Protective, restrained, privacy-first |

### 3. Pacing

Plan a meaningful visual, text, audio, or emotional change every 2-3 seconds.
Use concrete verbs and visual nouns:

- "fired back"
- "broke down"
- "walked out"
- "sold out"
- "turned the rumor into content"
- "the story flipped"

Do not add visual clutter without narrative purpose. Each shot should advance
the conflict, evidence, emotional shift, or payoff.

### 4. Engagement Trigger

End with a decision, prediction, genuine uncertainty, identity response, or
useful public question.

Strong directions:

- "Was this self-defense or excessive force?"
- "Where should the line be drawn?"
- "Which fact should investigators release first?"
- "Genius move or avoidable mistake?"
- "Would you have shared this before checking?"

Avoid:

- "Follow for more."
- "What do you think?" with no focus.
- Forcing a binary in a complex legal, health, abuse, or grief story.
- Informing accusations, doxxing, or harassment.

## S.T.O.R.M. Editing Structure

Use this as a pacing and retention guide, not a rigid transcript.

| Beat | Purpose | Typical Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shocking Hook | Stop the scroll with one supported conflict, result, quote, or visual | 0-3s |
| The Context | Explain only what the audience needs to understand the conflict | 3-15s |
| Opposition | Escalate the opposing claims, stakes, timeline, or consequences | 15-30s |
| Reframe | Reveal the key distinction, turn, or interpretation | 30-45s |
| Message & CTA | Give the audience one useful question or decision to debate | 45-60s |

For 80-second scripts, extend the context and opposition beats rather than
adding filler or repeating the hook.

## Information Density

For news, explainers, and commentary, add a meaningful evidence point roughly
every 5-8 seconds. Evidence points include:

- A date, number, or result
- A direct quote
- A document, video, or source detail
- A change in the timeline
- A clear contrast between competing claims

Evidence points do not need to be dramatic. They need to move the viewer
forward and prevent repetition.

## Share and Save Logic

Identify why someone would share or save the video:

- Share: public-safety value, moral clarity, useful warning, shocking evidence,
  or a question a friend would want to debate
- Save: timeline, legal explainer, evidence checklist, future update, or
  practical lesson

If there is no share or save reason, improve the substance rather than adding
an artificial CTA.

## Platform Signals

TikTok does not publish a complete ranking formula. Treat the following as
optimization signals, not secrets or guarantees:

- First-3-second retention and continued watching
- Average watch time and completion
- Rewatches
- Shares and saves
- Comments and replies
- Follows
- Negative feedback such as "not interested" or reports

Optimize the script for one clear viewer promise and a strong opening, but do
not keyword-stuff, promise a result, or use engagement bait that harms trust.

## Comment Mining

After publishing, use comments to identify the next useful video:

- Repeated factual questions
- Unresolved legal or timeline questions
- Strong disagreements that reveal a real information gap
- Requests for a source, update, or comparison

Do not treat viral comments or accusations as evidence. Verify before turning
a comment into a new claim.

## Rewrite Sequence

1. Delete the first two sentences if they are setup.
2. Move the strongest confirmed detail into the first sentence.
3. Replace abstract verbs with concrete actions.
4. Remove repeated background, repeated allegations, and generic transitions.
5. Add one visual or audio cut for each beat.
6. Confirm that the CTA matches the actual uncertainty.
7. For social news, run the legal and source checks again.

## Safety Overrides

The following override the viral template:

- Do not invent conflict, motive, relationships, injuries, or evidence.
- Do not present an allegation, arrest, lawsuit, or rumor as a conviction.
- Do not use the phrase or idea of a "haunted" claim as fact.
- Do not expose minors, victims, private addresses, schools, or intimate
  material.
- Do not turn mental health, suicide, overdose, or grief into a spectacle.
- Do not use a provocative question to encourage harassment.
- Do not claim a format, sound, or hook guarantees distribution.

## Review Output

When the user asks for a diagnostic review, return:

1. Hook score and diagnosis.
2. Pacing and language issues.
3. Fact and legal risk.
4. CTA diagnosis.
5. A rewritten version.
6. A short note explaining what changed and why.
