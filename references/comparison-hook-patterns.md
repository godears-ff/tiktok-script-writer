# Comparison Hook Patterns

Use this P0 reference for choice, decision, public-figure, A-versus-B,
expected-versus-actual, before-versus-after, and status-gap topics.

The goal is to create a clear question, a fast answer axis, and a concrete
contrast without inventing a decision, motive, relationship, or private fact.

## When to Use

Use comparison framing when the source supports at least one of these:

- A person or brand chose one option over another.
- Two people, brands, paths, or outcomes are being compared.
- The expected explanation differs from the evidence.
- A public behavior changed over time.
- There is a visible gap in status, resources, rules, risk, or consequence.

Do not manufacture a comparison just because it sounds dramatic. If the
premise is only a rumor, label it as a rumor and make the uncertainty part of
the question.

## Question Hook Bank

Choose one primary question. Keep the premise factual, reported, alleged, or
explicitly framed as audience speculation.

### Choice or Decision

- "Why did [A] choose [B] over [C]?"
- "Why did [A] reportedly turn down [B]?"
- "[A] had two options. Why this one?"
- "[A] didn't take the obvious route. Why?"

### Change or Reversal

- "Why has [A] stopped doing [B]?"
- "[A] used to [old behavior]. What changed?"
- "The early story was [X]. The latest evidence says [Y]."
- "This looked like [expected explanation]. Then [new fact] changed the frame."

### Hidden Stakes

- "The real issue isn't [X]. It's [Y]."
- "Everyone focused on [surface detail]. The bigger question is [underlying issue]."
- "[A] says [stated reason]. The record shows [supported contrast]."
- "The question isn't whether [A] happened. It's why [B] followed."

### Status or Resource Gap

- "[A] lives in one world. [B] lives in another."
- "[A] has [resource]. [B] has [constraint]. That changes the choice."
- "One side risks money. The other risks [different consequence]."

### Tradeoff or Judgment

- "[A] didn't just do [X]. They chose [Y]."
- "Was this [A], [B], or both?"
- "What matters more here: [value A] or [value B]?"
- "Was the brand protecting [A] or avoiding [B]?"

Use quotation marks only around the exact question structure, not as a claim.
Rewrite the question to fit the creator's voice and the verified record.

## Contrast Matrix

Build the comparison before writing the script. Use two to five rows that
change the viewer's understanding.

| Dimension | Side A / Expected | Side B / Actual | Evidence or Source | Qualifier |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------------|-----------|
| Status or role | [fact] | [fact] | [source] | confirmed / reported / disputed / unknown |
| Choice or behavior | [fact] | [fact] | [source] | confirmed / reported / disputed / unknown |
| Resources or cost | [fact] | [fact] | [source] | confirmed / reported / disputed / unknown |
| Consequence or tradeoff | [fact] | [fact] | [source] | confirmed / reported / disputed / unknown |
| Unresolved question | [what is not known] | [what would change the interpretation] | [source gap] | unknown |

### Matrix Rules

- Every material claim needs a source or an explicit qualifier.
- Do not compare a confirmed fact against a rumor as if they have equal
  evidentiary weight.
- A contrast must change the interpretation, stakes, or decision. Delete
  decorative contrasts.
- Do not expose private information, intimate material, minors, medical
  details, or unverified relationships for shock value.
- Do not use body measurements, sexual history, or private motives as the
  primary proof point.
- If the comparison cannot be supported, use a result, evidence, or
  unresolved-question hook instead.

## Answer Axis (0:00-0:05)

The hook creates the question. The answer axis tells viewers what kind of
answer is coming.

It must appear by 0:05, usually in the second or third spoken sentence.

Good answer axes:

- "The real issue isn't age. It's money."
- "This wasn't a standard endorsement. She got equity."
- "The decision looks personal, but the evidence points to risk."
- "The question isn't what fans believe. It's what the record can prove."

Weak answer axes:

- "Here's why."
- "It gets complicated."
- "You won't believe what happened."
- "There are many sides to this story."

The answer axis may reveal a distinction, tradeoff, contradiction, or decision
frame. It must not reveal a false conclusion or assert an unverified motive.

## Safe Intensity

Keep the tension in the public decision, documented contrast, or unresolved
question, not in an accusation.

Prefer:

- "The report claims..."
- "The footage shows..."
- "The public record does not confirm..."
- "Fans believe..."
- "What remains unknown is..."

Avoid:

- Stating a private motive as fact.
- Saying someone "rejected" or "chose" another person without a source.
- Using "ensures," "proves," or "everyone knows" for disputed claims.
- Turning a rumor into a question that implies guilt or blame.

## Rewrite Sequence

1. Identify the viewer's existing question.
2. Verify that the question's premise is supported.
3. Build the contrast matrix.
4. Choose the strongest supported question hook.
5. Write the answer axis for 0:05.
6. Draft the body around three to five evidence points.
7. End with a decision, prediction, tradeoff, or genuinely useful question.
8. Recheck that the Chinese translation preserves the same uncertainty.
