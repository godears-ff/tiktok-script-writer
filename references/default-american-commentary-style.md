# Default American Commentary Style

Use this reference for new scripts unless the user explicitly asks for a
neutral news brief, official announcement, or another format.

## Core Direction

The default output is US-targeted commentary, not a news wire:

- The creator has a point of view.
- Facts support the reaction.
- The narration sounds like a person talking to viewers, not reading a report.
- Curiosity, surprise, debate, gossip, and controlled exaggeration are
  encouraged when the subject supports them.
- The viewer should feel invited into the conversation.

## Voice Markers

Use natural phrases such as:

- "Here's the part that gets me."
- "I'm not buying that."
- "This is where it gets messy."
- "The internet ran with it."
- "You can't tell me that's not wild."
- "I get why fans are split."
- "That's not just embarrassing. That's a whole storyline."

Use them as writing patterns, not as mandatory catchphrases.

## News-to-Commentary Conversion

Do not simply read the article in order.

1. Extract the fact ledger.
2. Find the most interesting contradiction, decision, quote, or consequence.
3. Open with that moment.
4. React to it.
5. Give only the context needed to understand the reaction.
6. Add evidence and competing interpretations.
7. End with a real debate, prediction, or judgment call.

Example:

- News brief: "The court set bond at $3,500."
- Commentary: "Three charges, a $3,500 bond, and still no verdict. That's the part everyone needs to keep straight."

## Personal Stance

Each script should have a stance, such as:

- Skeptical
- Curious
- Impressed
- Disappointed
- Conflicted
- Amused
- Frustrated
- Hopeful

The stance can change after new evidence, but it should be visible.

## Exaggeration Rules

Allowed:

- Emotional exaggeration: "This whole thing is a circus."
- Rhetorical contrast: "This is less a breakup and more a brand rollout."
- Playful judgment: "That has to be the messiest way to handle this."
- Meme framing: "This is a reality-show cliffhanger."

Not allowed:

- Changing a number, date, quote, or legal stage
- Saying an allegation is proven
- Inventing private motives as fact
- Claiming someone is guilty because the internet says so
- Turning death, abuse, health, or minor-related harm into a joke

## Sensitive-Topic Adjustment

For death, abuse, health, minors, and active legal cases:

- Keep the personal voice and clear point of view.
- Remove playful exaggeration.
- Use calm skepticism rather than mockery.
- Keep every legal and factual qualifier.
- Replace jokes with empathy, accountability, or a public question.

## CTA Default

Prefer a real discussion trigger:

- "Do you buy this explanation?"
- "Was this smart, messy, or both?"
- "Who is actually getting played here?"
- "Is this a real conflict or just content?"
- "Would you have handled it differently?"

Do not force a binary when the issue is genuinely complex.

## Final Check

- Does it sound like a US creator speaking?
- Does it have a personal point of view?
- Is the reaction more prominent than the chronology?
- Are the facts still exact?
- Would viewers have a reason to comment?
