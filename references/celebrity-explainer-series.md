# Celebrity Explainer Series

Use this P1 mode automatically when the source primarily concerns a celebrity,
influencer, streamer, actor, singer, athlete, royal, creator, entertainment
figure, or other public personality.

The default topic is the person's public decision, relationship, brand deal,
career move, money, image, statement, controversy, or timeline.

## Automatic Trigger

Select this mode when the source subject is a celebrity, influencer,
entertainer, or public figure and the main question is about what they did,
said, chose, changed, received, risked, or publicly responded to.

Do not select this mode when:

1. The user explicitly requests another format or neutral news brief.
2. The primary material is film, TV, sports, or performance commentary rather
   than the person's decision or public behavior.
3. Death, abuse, health, minors, active legal proceedings, criminal
   allegations, or another high-risk issue controls the story.

In an override, the higher-risk mode controls facts and language. This series
may supply only a safe structural or tonal layer.

## Six-Beat Series Structure

Use these six beats in order. The exact timing can scale to 30, 60, or 80
seconds, but no beat may be replaced by a chronological biography.

| Beat | 80s Guide | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 1. Question hook | 0:00-0:03 | Use the viewer's already-existing question when supported |
| 2. Answer axis | 0:03-0:07 | State the distinction, tradeoff, or contradiction to be explained |
| 3. Three contrast layers | 0:07-0:40 | Expected vs actual; A vs B; cost vs consequence |
| 4. Receipts or qualifiers | 0:40-0:56 | Show the evidence and clearly mark what is reported, disputed, or unknown |
| 5. Judgment | 0:56-0:70 | Give the creator's visible stance without pretending to know private motive |
| 6. Decision CTA | 0:70-0:80 | Ask a topic-specific value, prediction, or tradeoff question |

### Contrast Layer 1: Expected vs Actual

Set up the surface explanation, then show the supported detail that changes
the interpretation.

Examples:

- "The headline says the decision was about one thing. The record points to another."
- "This looks like a personal choice until the money and control are placed side by side."

### Contrast Layer 2: A vs B

Compare two people, options, brands, paths, or outcomes only when the source
supports the comparison.

Useful dimensions:

- Status or role
- Stated or visible choice
- Resources or opportunity
- Risk or cost
- Public consequence

### Contrast Layer 3: Cost vs Consequence

Explain what each side gains, gives up, controls, or risks. This is where the
series turns facts into a decision rather than a gossip recap.

## Motive-Safe Phrasing

Prefer:

- "The report claims..."
- "The public record shows..."
- "Fans believe..."
- "What remains unknown is..."
- "The decision could be read as..."
- "There is no public evidence that..."

Avoid:

- "He only did it because..."
- "She rejected him to protect her image."
- "This proves they are..."
- "Everyone knows the real reason..."
- "The relationship is fake."
- "He is clearly using her."

Private motive, intimate history, body measurements, sexual history, and
family conflict are not proof. Use them only when essential, public, and
properly qualified.

## Subtype CTA Routing

Match the ending question to the actual public decision.

| Story Type | CTA Direction |
|------------|---------------|
| Relationship | Was this personal, strategic, or both? |
| Brand deal | Smart partnership, brand mismatch, or pure attention play? |
| Career transition | Strong career move, short-term attention, or too risky? |
| Money or assets | Reasonable boundary, public overreach, or unresolved legal question? |
| Feud or conflict | Who had the stronger public move, and what evidence supports it? |
| Image or style shift | Intentional rebrand, natural change, or audience repositioning? |
| Rumor | What evidence would change how viewers interpret this? |
| Public statement | Was the response clear, evasive, or strategically incomplete? |

Do not ask viewers to judge appearance, sexual behavior, private grief, or a
person's worth.

## Series Consistency

Keep the same series shell across episodes:

- One recurring title or verbal label
- One question-first opening pattern
- One to three contrast layers
- One explicit evidence or qualifier beat
- One visible judgment
- One subtype-specific CTA

The content can vary. The structure should not.

## Fact and Source Rules

- Separate confirmed facts, reported claims, disputed claims, and unknowns.
- Do not turn a rumor, fan theory, private motive, or body detail into proof.
- Do not present an engagement ring, outfit, photo, or social-media post as
  automatic confirmation of a relationship or decision.
- If the source is a social post or transcript, state the source basis and
  verify names, dates, and direct quotes before publication.
- Use the exact legal stage when a celebrity story intersects with a case.
